// Chapter 01 — The part nobody explains

Your website lives on someone else's computer

A website has two separate parts. The domain is the address, like yourbusiness.co.za. The hosting is the computer that holds your pages, pictures and emails and shows them to visitors. Most owners pay for both without ever thinking about the second one, until the site goes down, gets hacked or turns slow.

Hosting matters more than it looks. If the computer is far away, local customers wait longer for your pages. If the host does not keep backups, one bad day can wipe your site. And if the host does not update the software your site runs on, you are exposed. That last point is not theoretical. This week South Africa's Presidency took its own website offline over a serious flaw in PHP, the software behind most WordPress sites. Read what happened and what to check on your site.

You do not need the biggest host. You need one that answers the phone when something breaks.
// Chapter 02 — The checklist

Five questions to ask any hosting company

Whoever you choose, ask these before you pay. A good host answers them in plain language.

01Where
Where are the servers?
For South African customers, servers in South Africa mean faster pages and your data stays in the country.
02Backups
Are there daily backups, and can you restore one?
A backup you cannot restore is not a backup. Ask how long a restore takes and who does it.
03Support
Who answers when something breaks?
Look for live chat or a phone number, not only a ticket form that gets answered on Monday.
04Updates
Who keeps the software up to date?
Ask which PHP version your site runs on and who updates it when a serious flaw is found.
05Exit
Can you leave with everything?
Keep your domain registered in your own name, and make sure you can download your files whenever you like.

// Chapter 05 — An honest fit check

Who it suits, and who it does not

It suits a typical business website: a brochure site, a booking page, a small online shop, or just email on your own domain. If you run WordPress, one-click installs and daily backups take a lot of weight off your shoulders.

It does not suit everything. A custom platform with logins, client data and strict rules about who can see what, such as a portal or a multi-branch system, is better built on purpose-made cloud infrastructure than on a shared hosting plan. That is the kind of work we do at Forge Vertical, and it is a different job from hosting a brochure site. I use both myself: TripSpace Global, our marketplace, runs on Google Cloud, while my email stays on Webway's cPanel. Each is the right tool for its job. If you are not sure which one you need, ask us and we will tell you honestly, even if the answer is a cheap shared plan.

Do not move in a hurry. Before you change host, take a full backup of your site and email, and check that your domain is registered in your name. Changing host does not change your domain, so you stay in control.
