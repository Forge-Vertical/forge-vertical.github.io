The Presidency's website is offline.
Here is what we know.

A global flaw in PHP has already been used in attacks. South Africa's Presidency took its site offline as a precaution. A breach has not been confirmed, but your own website may need an update today.

Jarrit Hosking
Forge Vertical · Cape Town · October 24, 2026
6 min read

Was South Africa's Presidency website hacked? That has not been confirmed. On 23 October 2026 the Presidency said its website was "temporarily unavailable as a precautionary measure in response to a global cybersecurity event." It did not say it had been breached. The event is a pair of critical flaws in PHP, the software that runs a large share of the world's websites, and attackers are already using them.

// Chapter 01 — What happened

A site taken offline as a precaution

On Friday 23 October the Presidency posted on X that its website was down while it responded to a global cybersecurity event. A few hours later, Daily Maverick reported that the site was still unreachable. The Presidency did not say how long the outage would last. Daily Maverick links the outage to the PHP flaws, but the Presidency has not confirmed that link.

It is worth being clear about what we do and do not know. Taking a site offline before it is patched is a sensible choice, and it is the kind of step the FBI and the UK's National Cyber Security Centre have advised when an update is not yet possible. It is not proof of a breach.

// Chapter 02 — The flaw

Two critical holes in PHP

PHP is the programming language behind a huge part of the web. It is also what WordPress runs on, and WordPress powers around four in ten websites. Two flaws, tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, were disclosed this week. Researchers have nicknamed them PHP2Shell.

In plain English: an attacker who can reach the server may be able to run their own commands on it, without a username or password. That can mean stolen data, defaced pages or a base for further attacks. Both flaws are rated 9.2 out of 10 for severity.

No setting change fully closes this one. Updating PHP is the only permanent fix.
// Chapter 03 — Is it being used?

Yes. Attacks have already been seen

Security firm watchTowr reported seeing attackers use both flaws on 21 October, the day patches were released. New Zealand's cyber security centre says it is aware of reports of active exploitation. BleepingComputer reports that Cloudflare's CTO said the company saw early attacks even before the patches shipped. VirusTotal estimates more than five million PHP hosts are exposed online.

None of that means your website has been attacked. It does mean that if your site runs on an unpatched version of PHP, you are in a race.

// Chapter 04 — What to do today

Check your PHP version, then update

If your website is built on WordPress, or any other PHP system, the PHP version is controlled by your hosting provider or your developer. Ask them today which version your site runs and whether it has been updated. These are the fixed versions:

// PHP 8.5 Update to 8.5.12 or newer
Fixed in the emergency release of 21 October.
// PHP 8.4 Update to 8.4.27 or newer
Fixed in the emergency release of 21 October.
// PHP 8.3 Update to 8.3.36 or newer
Fixed in the emergency release of 21 October.
// PHP 8.2 Update to 8.2.35 or newer
Fixed in the emergency release of 21 October.
Running PHP 8.1 or older? Those versions no longer get official updates, so there is no standard fix. Ask your host for a patched package from your provider, or move to a supported version. Many older small-business sites sit on old PHP without anyone realising.

After the update, the workers that run PHP need to be restarted, so ask your host to confirm that has happened. If you cannot update straight away, the FBI and NCSC advice is to limit access to the site, or take it offline until it is patched. If you think your site may already have been touched, keep your logs and do not delete anything before someone has looked at it.

// Chapter 05 — The wider lesson

Assume one day a server will be taken over

Patches protect you against a flaw that has a name. The next flaw may not have one yet. So the useful question is not only "are we updated?" but "if someone did get in, what could they reach?"

That is where controlling who can see what matters. When customer information is separated, each part of the system only reaches what it needs, and every access is recorded, a break-in has less to find and is far easier to spot. It does not stop an attack like this one. It limits the damage and helps you answer the questions afterwards.

// Questions answered

Was the South African Presidency's website hacked?

Not confirmed. The Presidency said its website was taken offline as a precaution in response to a global cybersecurity event. It has not said it was breached. Daily Maverick links the outage to critical PHP flaws, but the Presidency has not confirmed that link.

What are the PHP flaws?

They are two critical vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, that can let an attacker run commands on a server without a username or password. Fixed versions are PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35, released on 21 October 2026.

Is my website affected?

It may be if it runs on PHP, which includes WordPress sites. Ask your hosting provider or developer which PHP version your site uses and whether it has been updated to a fixed release. Versions 8.1 and older have no official fix.

How do I find out my PHP version?

Ask your hosting provider or developer. On many hosting control panels, a tool such as the PHP version manager shows it. If you are not sure, send us your website address and we will tell you what we can see.

Do I need to take my website offline?

If you cannot update straight away, the FBI and the UK's NCSC advise limiting access or taking the affected system offline until it is patched. The best step is to ask your host to update PHP as soon as possible.
