I built a free extension that
hides AI-labelled posts.

A number of people said they'd pay for something that clears AI-generated content out of their feed. So I built the simplest version that could actually work — one that uses the AI-content labels Facebook, Instagram, Google, and LinkedIn already apply, and just hides what they've already flagged.

Jarrit Hosking
Forge Vertical · Cape Town · November 7, 2026
6 min read
// Where this came from

I kept seeing the same comment on Facebook — people saying they'd genuinely pay for a tool that cleared AI-generated images, video, and text out of their feed. Not because they're against AI, but because they can't always tell the difference anymore, and that uncertainty bothers them more than the content itself.

I didn't want to build this as a business. It costs me time, not money, and if someone wants to send a few rand my way for the effort, that's a bonus, not the point. So the brief I set myself was: build the simplest version that actually works, ship it, and see if there's real demand before building anything more complicated.

The honest version of "AI detection" in this extension: it does not run its own AI model, image analysis, or OCR. It reads the labels the platforms themselves already attach to AI-generated or AI-edited content, and hides the post. That's it. It's a filter on top of work Meta, Google, and LinkedIn are already doing — not a replacement for it.

What it actually catches

// Platform coverage, version 1

Each platform signals AI content differently, and none of them documents it publicly, so this came from directly testing each one and combing through what worked on one to check against the next.

// Facebook & Instagram
Meta's "AI info" label
Catches posts Meta has tagged with its own AI-generated or AI-edited label. Works well on obvious cases; Meta sometimes buries the label in a post's menu rather than showing it inline, which the extension can't see yet.
// Google Search & Images
"AI Generated" tag
Hides results and images Google itself has marked as AI-generated in search and image search.
// LinkedIn
AI content icon
Detects LinkedIn's own AI-content indicator on posts and images in the feed.
// Not yet covered
Unlabelled content, Reddit
Anything a platform hasn't labelled itself won't be caught in version 1 — including Reddit, which was deliberately left out for now. No platform catches everything.

Why I didn't build a custom AI detector

// The actual trade-off

A proper AI-image classifier, OCR pass, and text-pattern model is a real piece of engineering — and a real ongoing cost to run. Before spending that time, I wanted to know whether people would actually use a free version long enough to tell me what they want from a better one.

That's what the feedback box in the extension's popup is for. It asks, in effect: is this useful as it is, what's missing, and would you pay for a version that catches more? Every submission goes straight into a database I can review — anonymously, no account or identity attached. If enough people say yes, a paid version with real detection is the obvious next step. If they don't, I've learned that for the cost of a weekend, not a quarter.

It's not catching everything. It's catching enough that a good number of people will be happy to have the worst of it gone — and that's a fair place to start.

No ads, no pay-to-remove-ads

// How it's funded

The extension carries a small "Built by Forge Vertical" credit in its popup, with a link back here for anyone who wants something similar built for them. There's also an optional support link for anyone who wants to buy me a coffee for the time it took. Neither is required, neither is removable for a fee, and there are no ads anywhere in it. If it ever grows into a paid tier, that will be a genuinely better product — OCR, image classification, broader coverage — not the free version held hostage.
