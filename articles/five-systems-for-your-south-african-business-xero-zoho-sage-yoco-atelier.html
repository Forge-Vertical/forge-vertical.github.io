5 systems to use for your business,
and what each one is really for.

Xero, Zoho Books, Sage, Yoco and Atelier OS: what they cost, what they do, and which combination fits.

Jarrit Hosking
Forge Vertical · Cape Town · November 19, 2026
9 min read

Which systems should a South African business use? Five worth knowing in 2026: Xero, Zoho Books and Sage Accounting for the books, Yoco for getting paid, and Atelier OS for quoting, invoicing and running the day-to-day. They do different jobs, so the right answer is usually a combination, not a single winner, and Atelier OS is not trying to compete with the others: it is built to sit alongside them. I built Atelier OS, so weigh my view of it accordingly. Every price below comes from the vendor's own page, checked on 19 November 2026.

01 / How to choose

Start with the job, not the brand

Most arguments about business software compare things that do not do the same job. A card machine is not an accounting package, and an accounting package is not the thing your team opens when a customer asks for a quote at eight in the morning.

Think of three layers. The front is where customers meet you: quotes, invoices, payment links. The middle is how the work and the people are run: who did what, who can see what. The back is the ledger: bank reconciliation, VAT returns and the numbers your bookkeeper needs. The five systems below sit at different points on that line.

02 / Xero

1. Xero: the accounting standard

Xero lists three plans in rand: Starter R485, Standard R857 and Premium R1,195 a month, stated including VAT, with a 30-day trial and a discounted first three months for new customers, per Xero's pricing page. Starter includes quotes and up to 20 invoices, online invoice payments, bank reconciliation and real-time reports. Standard removes the invoice cap and adds bulk reconciliation and custom dashboards. Premium adds multiple currencies and a longer cash-flow forecast.

Choose it if you want strong bank reconciliation and a large ecosystem of connected apps, or your bookkeeper already lives in it. Watch for the Starter invoice cap and extra charges for add-ons such as expense claims and project tracking. It is an accounting product, so trade-specific jobs are not its focus.

03 / Zoho Books

2. Zoho Books: breadth, and a free plan

Zoho lists a Free plan for one user and one accountant, with up to 1,000 invoices a year, for as long as financial-year revenue is under R1 million. Paid plans then step up: Standard R99, Professional R199, Premium R299, Elite R1,290 and Ultimate R2,490 a month, with annual billing lower, and prices stated as exclusive of local taxes, per Zoho's pricing page.

The plan structure matters. According to Zoho's own table, custom fields start at Standard, custom user roles at Professional, and custom modules and a custom domain at Premium. A Locations add-on is listed at R150 per location a month on annual billing.

Choose it if you want a wide feature set and are happy to grow into higher plans. Watch for the point where a feature you rely on sits one plan above the one you are paying for.

04 / Sage

3. Sage Accounting: the familiar local option

Sage lists Accounting Start at R270 a month for one user and Accounting Standard at R490 a month including two users, with prices stated including VAT and a 30-day free trial. Payroll is a separate R97 a month product, and add-ons are listed for extra users (R85), extra companies (R460), advanced inventory, time tracking and multi-currency.

Choose it if your accountant or bookkeeper prefers it, or you want payroll from the same vendor. Watch for add-on pricing, and note that Sage's pricing page does not list quote or invoice limits.

05 / Yoco

4. Yoco: getting paid, in person and online

Yoco is a payments company, not an accounting package. Its pricing page lists the Khumo 2 card machine at R699 and Khumo Print 2 at R1,399 once-off, with no monthly machine rental. Card fees depend on your monthly turnover and plan: on the Core plan (R0 a month) the in-person local rate starts at 2.30% for turnover up to R40,000, and the Pro plan (R499 a month) starts at 2.03%, with fees stated excluding VAT and the page noting its own conflicting figures above R50,000, so confirm on the page.

Payment links let you share a link by WhatsApp, SMS or email so a customer can pay by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay, with no website needed. Funds reach you within two business days, according to Yoco.

Choose it if customers pay you by card, at your premises or remotely. Watch for fees that move with your volume, and remember it does not run quotes, jobs or your books.

08 / Putting them together

Which combination fits which business

  • One person or a small trade business: Atelier OS on the free plan for quotes and invoices, a Yoco machine or payment link for card payments, and a bookkeeper working from downloaded reports.
  • A growing business with a bookkeeper: Atelier OS for the front, and Xero, Zoho Books or Sage for the ledger, whichever your bookkeeper prefers.
  • A business with branches or franchisees: ask every system the three questions below. Atelier runs branches from one admin account with billing per branch, so put the questions to it too.
  • Payroll and heavy accounting: one of the three accounting products. This is what they are for.
09 / Three questions to ask any system

Who sees what, and who can change it?

If you have more than one location, branch or franchisee, ask these before you sign anything:

  1. Can one branch see another branch's data? It should be no.
  2. Can anyone change a document without head office knowing? It should be no, or at least recorded.
  3. Can I get all my data out if I leave? It should be yes.

Ask for a demonstration, not a promise. It takes ten minutes and it settles most doubts.

// Questions answered

What is the best accounting software for a small business in South Africa?

There is no single best. Xero, Zoho Books and Sage Accounting are all established and all publish prices in rand. Choose by the job: Xero for bank reconciliation and its app ecosystem, Zoho Books for its breadth and a free plan for very small businesses, and Sage for local accounting with payroll available as a separate product. Start with what your bookkeeper already uses.

Do I need accounting software if I only send quotes and invoices?

Quotes and invoices are the front of the business, and accounting is the ledger behind it. Many small businesses start with quoting, invoicing and getting paid, then add accounting when their bookkeeper needs a full ledger. A system that lets you download reports makes that hand-over simple.

How much does Xero cost in South Africa?

As published on 19 November 2026, Xero lists Starter at R485, Standard at R857 and Premium at R1,195 a month, stated in ZAR including VAT, with a 30-day trial and an introductory discount for new customers. Starter includes quotes and up to 20 invoices a month. Xero says it can change prices at any time.

Is Zoho Books free in South Africa?

Zoho lists a Free plan for one user plus one accountant, with up to 1,000 invoices a year, available while the financial-year revenue stays under R1 million. Paid plans run from Standard at R99 a month to Ultimate at R2,490 a month, and Zoho states its prices are exclusive of local taxes.

What does Atelier OS cost?

Atelier has three plans. Free is R0 and includes 50 quotes and 6 invoices a month, with Atelier branding on documents. Individual is R599 a month with unlimited quotes and invoices, your own branding and Store plugins. Franchise is R900 a month plus R900 a month per branch, with unlimited quotes and invoices, a franchise admin dashboard and guided data migration. Store plugins are priced separately, from free to R150 a month, with SMS Notifications from R200 a month.

Can Atelier OS replace my accounting software?

Not today. Atelier OS covers quoting, invoicing and collecting payment, with report downloads your bookkeeper can use. It is built to sit in front of the books, not to replace a full ledger.
