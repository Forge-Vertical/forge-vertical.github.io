// The number Booking.com does not advertise

Booking.com is the world's largest accommodation marketplace. 4.7 million properties. Over 500 million registered users. Used by travellers in every country on earth. It is extraordinary infrastructure and it deserves credit for what it built.

It is also a machine that extracts 10–25% of every booking from the people who actually own and operate the properties. (cite index="52-1">Booking.com charges hosts a commission of 10-25% per booking, with a global average of approximately 15%. Additional payment processing fees of 1.1-3.1% apply if you use Payments by Booking.com. The Preferred Partner programme — which gives properties better visibility — adds approximately 3% extra commission on top of that.

That means a host participating in Preferred Partner, using Booking's payment processing, paying at the average commission rate, is handing over approximately 19–21% of every booking to the platform. Not 15%. Nineteen to twenty-one percent.

Let that number sit for a moment. For every R10,000 booking, the host keeps roughly R7,900 to R8,100 — before their own operational costs.

Why OTA commission is the hospitality industry's biggest unspoken problem

// Chapter 03 — The structural issue

The OTA commission model has been normalised over two decades to the point where many hosts consider it the cost of doing business. It is not. It is a structural extraction that compounds over time and limits what hospitality operators can reinvest in their properties, their staff, and their guests.

(cite index="57-1">In 2026, property owners using Booking.com face 15–23% commission, additional 1.1–3.1% payment processing fees, and the impact of the new relevance-based Genius algorithm, which can require paying more to maintain previous visibility levels. The algorithm change is the tell. The platform is not just taking commission — it is increasingly structuring the system so that maintaining visibility requires paying more commission. Pay to be found, then pay when you are found.

(cite index="50-1">Booking.com's own SEC filing for Q2 2026 acknowledges that SEO traffic is declining and that this may lead to increased spend in paid marketing channels. Read that clearly: Booking.com is telling investors that organic search traffic is falling and they plan to spend more on paid advertising to compensate. That paid advertising spend is funded by host commissions. Hosts are paying for Booking.com's Google ads. Not hypothetically — structurally.

The Genius algorithm change — what it actually means for hosts: Booking.com's Genius loyalty programme was overhauled in early 2026. Previously, properties received guaranteed visibility benefits from participation. After the overhaul, visibility became relevance-based — meaning the platform now controls what "relevant" means, and paying more commission is one of the factors that influences it. Hosts are being asked to pay more to maintain the same position they previously had.

The commission-free argument — why it is not just about saving money

// Chapter 04 — What 0% enables

The financial argument for commission-free booking is obvious. The strategic argument is more important.

When a host books through Booking.com, the platform owns the guest relationship. Booking.com has the guest's email address, payment details, travel history, and booking preferences. The host gets a name and a check-in date. When that guest wants to book again, they go back to Booking.com — not back to the host. The commission is not just a fee. It is the price of renting access to your own guests.

Commission-free direct booking — whether through a platform like TripSpace Global or a host's own website — returns that relationship to the host. A guest who books direct is a guest the host can contact, retain, and build loyalty with. Over time, a direct booking database is worth more than any individual booking commission saved. It is the difference between renting customers and owning them.

(cite index="59-1">Your own direct booking channel is the ultimate alternative for achieving 100% control, building your brand, and eliminating commission fees. TripSpace Global is the marketplace layer that sits between "Booking.com" and "build your own booking website" — a commission-free platform with direct host-guest relationships and no per-booking extraction.

The honest position — you probably still need Booking.com

// Chapter 05 — A fair take

Booking.com's reach is real. 500 million registered users. Dominant search presence. Deep trust from international travellers. A property that relies entirely on direct bookings and zero OTA presence is leaving significant occupancy on the table, particularly for guests booking internationally where they do not know local platforms.

The smart strategy is not abandonment — it is balance. Use Booking.com for the volume and the international reach. Use TripSpace Global and your own direct booking channel to own the relationships that volume generates. Every guest who books through Booking.com and then books direct the second time is a guest you converted from 15% cost to 0% cost. That conversion compounds.

The hospitality operators who will win the next decade are not the ones who are most visible on Booking.com. They are the ones who use Booking.com's visibility to acquire guests, and then convert those guests into direct relationships that the platform cannot touch.

How much commission does Booking.com charge hosts?

(cite index="52-1">Commission is charged even on cancellations and no-shows unless you mark the guest as a no-show in the Extranet within 48 hours of planned check-out. This is one of the less-discussed costs — hosts pay commission on revenue they never actually received.
