// Who Arun Maini actually is

The name on the channel is MrWhosTheBoss. The person behind it is Arun Rupesh Maini — born October 24, 1995, in Nottingham, England. British-Indian. Graduate of Nottingham High School and then the University of Warwick, where he completed a BSc in Economics. He married Dhrisha Mehta in 2024 and announced in April 2026 that they are expecting their first child.

He started his YouTube channel in 2011 — the same year Snapchat launched, the same year the iPhone 4S introduced Siri. He was 15 years old. He did not have a dedicated camera, a studio, or a team. He had a budget smartphone and, by his own account, a goal to be the boss of his own destiny. The name of the channel is a statement of intent that took fifteen years to fully justify.

22.8 million subscribers. 8.9 billion views. A secondary channel — Mrwhosetheboss Plus — with 6.9 million subscribers of its own. An Instagram following of 2.5 million. His videos average 4.1 million views. His engagement rate of 3.02% is above the technology YouTube category norm, which sits closer to 2-3%. These are not accidental numbers. They are the result of a specific content philosophy that the rest of the tech creator ecosystem has spent a decade studying without fully replicating.

The content philosophy that built 8.9 billion views

// Chapter 01 — What he does differently

Most tech reviewers cover specifications. Arun covers the experience of owning technology — and specifically the experience a non-technical person would have. His flagship reviews regularly run 25 to 35 minutes, which is counterintuitive in an attention economy that rewards brevity. The length works because his audience is not casual — they are actively evaluating purchase decisions and they want someone they trust to have already done the thinking.

While most creators focus on specs, Arun focuses on spectacle. His biggest videos are not spec comparisons — they are events. Building the world's largest functional iPhone. Testing smartphones by running over them with cars. Giving away devices worth thousands of dollars to strangers. The spectacle is not separate from the review — it is the vehicle for it. You stay for the destruction test. You trust the review conclusion because you watched him earn it.

His brand deal history includes OnePlus and Samsung — smartphone OEMs who treat a MrWhosTheBoss video as a meaningful part of their global launch strategy, not a bonus placement, but a primary channel for reaching the English-speaking premium consumer technology buyer. That status — sought out by the brands themselves rather than chasing deals — is what fifteen years of consistent quality builds.

You stay for the destruction test. You trust the review conclusion because you watched him earn it. That is the entire content philosophy in two sentences.

The US border detention — what happened

// Chapter 03 — May 2026

In May 2026, Arun Maini disclosed in an interview with Roya News that he had been detained, strip-searched, and deported by US border officials on a sponsored work trip. The detention left a permanent flag on his immigration record.

He was travelling to the United States for a content creation project — the kind of trip that has been routine for creators of his scale for years. What happened at the border has not been fully detailed publicly, but the outcome is documented: detained, strip-searched, deported, flagged. For a British-Indian creator whose audience spans the US, UK, and global markets, and whose brand deal work frequently involves American companies, the implications are significant.

Arun addressed it publicly rather than quietly. That is consistent with how he has handled every difficult moment in fifteen years of public life — directly, on camera, with his audience. His willingness to share the uncomfortable parts of his reality alongside the records and the milestones is a large part of why 22.8 million people trust his opinion on a smartphone.

The broader context: The US border detention story landed in May 2026 — the same period that the US government was implementing tightened export controls on AI models and restricting access to frontier AI systems. The intersection of technology, international travel, and US federal authority is a recurring theme in 2026. For a tech creator whose work is built on covering that intersection, the experience carries an additional layer of relevance.

Why MrWhosTheBoss matters to the tech conversation

// Chapter 04 — The influence argument

There are bigger YouTube channels. There are channels with more niche technical depth. There are channels that break hardware news faster. What MrWhosTheBoss does that none of those channels do as well is translate technical decisions into human consequences — and do it for an audience of nearly 23 million people who are actively making purchase decisions.

When Arun says a phone camera is good, millions of people take that seriously. When he says a feature is gimmicky, millions of people factor that into their buying decision. Smartphone OEMs treat a MrWhosTheBoss video as a primary channel for reaching the English-speaking premium consumer technology buyer. That is not hyperbole — it is a documented reality of how tech brands allocate their creator budget.

He has also collaborated with the people who define the tech YouTube ecosystem — Marques Brownlee (MKBHD), iJustine, and Gaurav Chaudhary (Technical Guruji). In an ecosystem where creators are often reluctant to share audiences, Arun has been generous with collaboration in a way that has benefited both his channel and the broader community.

He is not the loudest voice in tech YouTube. He is one of the most trusted. Fifteen years of consistency — starting in an attic, building through health struggles, signing with management, hitting every milestone on the way to 22.8 million — has produced a creator whose endorsement carries genuine weight. That is a scarce thing. Most people cannot buy it. Arun earned it.

The 15-year lesson

// Chapter 06 — What the numbers actually mean

The mistake people make when they look at Arun Maini's subscriber count is treating it as an overnight outcome. 22.8 million subscribers is the result of fifteen years of publishing consistently, improving continuously, and treating the audience as people who deserve honest information rather than marketing copy dressed as reviews.

He published through the years when the channel was small. He published through health struggles he has talked about openly. He published when nobody was watching and he kept publishing as tens of millions started to. The compounding effect of fifteen years of consistent work on a single platform is what 8.9 billion views looks like.

The US border detention, the Guinness World Record, the Sidemen Charity Match penalty, the baby — these are the life of a person who has been building something real for fifteen years and is now living at the scale that kind of work eventually produces. Not every outcome is positive. The permanence of a US immigration flag is a serious consequence of whatever happened at that border. But the willingness to talk about it publicly, alongside the records and the wins, is what makes an audience of 22.8 million people feel like they know him. Because they do, in the way that fifteen years of honest content makes possible.
