The story of International Panel Shop.

It started with a Ford Fairlane, a price sucked out of a thumb, and a husband and wife who had not been in the trade for 20 years. What followed was one of Cape Town's most quietly remarkable small business stories — a 23-year journey from a backyard garage to an established panel beating operation with R2.23 million in assets, a 4.8-star reputation, and a business now available for acquisition.

// Chapter 01 — The Ford Fairlane

Lance had been out of the panel beating trade for twenty years. He had left it behind to work in drug rehabilitation — helping young people off Cape Town's streets, alongside his wife Serena. The money was not the point. The kids were the point.

But the bills did not care about the point. Seven years of financial strain — not knowing one day to the next how to pay the rent, the lights, the school fees. Lance and Serena had tried a cleaning product business called Quick & Easy, selling at flea markets on weekends and door-to-door during the week. It kept them going. It did not get them out.

Then came the Ford Fairlane.

Lance was delivering cleaning product to a mechanic client when he noticed a beautiful old blue Ford Fairlane in the driveway — freshly re-sprayed, but badly done. He said so. The client, who owned the Fairlane, asked: "Can you do a better job?" Lance had not given a quote in twenty years. He had not touched a spray gun professionally in two decades. But he said: "Of course." And when asked for a price, he pulled a number from nowhere — R2,000.

That weekend, he got to work. Over two weeks, he re-acquainted himself with a trade he had spent half his life away from — using the mechanic's equipment, in the mechanic's workshop, with the mechanic's customers gradually stopping coming in for mechanical repairs and starting to come in for body repairs instead.

Lance repaired nine vehicles that month. Including the Fairlane.

"With no marketing, word soon got around about this fellow called Lance who can do body repair work. The mechanic's customers were going to the shop looking for Lance, and the client was supplying them with his cell number." — Serena Hosking, founding account, 2021

From the garage to the first shop

// Chapter 02 — Lance's Panel & Spray

The money from those nine vehicles bought them something specific: a house with a decent-sized garage. That garage became the workshop. Lance taught Serena how to do body repairs. A husband-and-wife team, working from their backyard, building Lance's Panel & Spray from nothing.

The marketing strategy was a flier on every car windscreen in and around Cape Town — the Atlantic Seaboard, the Southern Suburbs, the CBD. Low-tech, high-volume, relentless. It worked. The work came. And eventually there was enough of it to justify a proper premises.

Their first shop: 8 Clare Street, Gardens, Cape Town. The new kids on the block, surrounded by panel shops the size of giants. Established operations with full complements of staff, the best equipment, and no reason to take a husband-and-wife team seriously.

They kept their heads down. They took every job the big shops did not want — the cars that could not afford the affluent rates. The work that nobody was fighting over. And they did it well enough that the customers who could only afford them started telling the customers who could afford anyone.

The word spread upward. The expensive cars followed. And one of the large, long-established shops in the area quietly closed. It was only at that point, Serena wrote, that they remembered to raise their heads.

What the business became

// Chapter 03 — International Panel Shop today

Twenty-three years of continuous trading. Two locations across the history of the business. Now based at 7 Bloem Street, Townsend Estate, Goodwood — a fully equipped auto body repair facility serving Cape Town and the broader Western Cape.

The operation handles the full spectrum of auto body work: collision repairs, paintless dent removal, chassis realignment, rust treatment, plastic bumper restoration, and full re-sprays. Premium paint from leading auto paint suppliers. A one-year warranty on all paintwork. The shop's approach to panel work is specific — they panel beat to the point before filler, so filler is applied liberally rather than as a substitute for proper metalwork, which means no cracking over time.

The business has developed a flexible payment approach that most panel shops in Cape Town have not matched. Customers can access vehicle repair financing through Mobicred — an established South African online credit provider — meaning repairs that would otherwise be unaffordable become accessible without the shop having to manage credit itself. Payment links are sent directly via WhatsApp or email. A buyer will set up their own payment processing accounts, but the system and the customer expectation of this kind of flexible payment option are already in place and working.

Rather than carrying a permanent employed workforce, the business operates on a contract basis — bringing in skilled technicians job by job from an established database of known contractors. The panel beating contractor market in Cape Town is well-serviced by platforms like panelbeatersdirectory.co.za, which maintains an active pool of trained technicians available for contract work. This means a buyer inherits a business without employment baggage — no permanent headcount obligations, no UIF exposure on staff, no retrenchment risk. The contractor relationships and the recruitment channels come with the business.

Why this business is genuinely hard to replicate

// Chapter 06 — The moat

It is worth being direct about what cannot be bought separately from this business, because it is the part that takes the longest to build and the part that matters most to the ongoing revenue.

The reputation is operational infrastructure. A 4.8-star Google rating built over 23 years, with review sentiment consistently pointing to technical quality and friendly service, directly influences how customers find and choose this business over competitors. It cannot be manufactured. It can only be maintained or destroyed. A buyer inherits a review profile that would take a new entrant a decade to approximate.

Insurance work exists but is not the primary driver. Some insurers have opted to refer work to International Panel Shop on an informal basis, and the team understands the process — documentation, assessor communication, repair standards — well enough to handle insurance jobs competently. However, the business is not formally registered on any insurer's approved panel, and insurance referrals do not form the bulk of the income. The primary revenue base is private-pay customers — which means the business is not dependent on insurer relationships to survive, and a buyer is not inheriting obligations or dependencies tied to panel registration status.

The supplier relationships affect quality and cost simultaneously. Premium auto paint from the very best auto paint suppliers is not just a quality claim — it reflects an established supply relationship with pricing and priority access that a new operation would not have from day one. Paint quality is one of the most visible differentiators in panel beating. Customers notice.

The location has proven customer access. Positioned in Goodwood at 3 Drukkery Road — now 7 Bloem Street, Townsend Estate — the business serves a catchment area that includes Goodwood, Milnerton, Parow, Bellville, and surrounding suburbs, while maintaining historical customer relationships from its Gardens location. The geographic reach is real and documented in the review geography.

The growth opportunities a buyer inherits

// Chapter 07 — What can be unlocked

The business as it stands is a proven, stable, cash-generating operation. But the current setup deliberately does not exploit several available growth vectors — leaving them intact for a buyer with capital, appetite, or specific industry expertise.

// 01 — Insurance panel approval
Formal insurer panel membership
Most major South African insurers operate approved panel networks. A buyer with the administrative infrastructure to pursue formal panel membership would access a significantly higher and more predictable volume of insurance-referred work.
// 02 — Fleet accounts
Corporate and government fleet contracts
The workshop infrastructure and throughput capacity can handle fleet volume. Corporate fleet contracts — with logistics companies, government departments, rental agencies — represent a reliable B2B income layer that the current operation has not formally pursued.
// 03 — AI damage assessment
Aurora Repair integration
Aurora Repair (aurora-repair.com) is an AI vehicle damage assessment tool built in partnership with the shop. A buyer who activates this tool creates a digital intake funnel — customers upload photos, receive instant assessments, and arrive at the workshop pre-qualified. It reduces quote time and increases conversion from inquiry to job.
// 04 — Capacity expansion
Second location or extended hours
The operational systems, supplier relationships, and brand equity are portable. A buyer with capital could replicate the operation at a second location, or increase throughput at the current location by scaling the contractor pool — something the lean current setup has not needed to pursue.

The honest picture — what a buyer needs to know

// Chapter 08 — Straightforward about the realities

This article is written to tell the story of the business accurately and to give a potential buyer a real picture of what they are acquiring. That means being direct about a few things.

Panel beating is a skills business. The quality of the work depends on the quality of the technicians. A buyer who comes from outside the industry will need to source contractors through the existing database and platforms like panelbeatersdirectory.co.za. The good news is that the contractor model removes the complexity of inheriting employed staff — a buyer can scale the contractor base up or down with work volume without fixed payroll obligations.

The price reflects 23 years of position, not just assets. The R2.23 million asset value is the floor. The asking price of R3.5 million reflects the brand, the review history, the customer relationships, the supplier access, and the 23-year trading track record. A buyer who understands those intangibles will see the value immediately. A buyer who only values the physical assets will find the price high.

Why this story matters beyond the sale

// Chapter 09 — What Lance and Serena actually built

Serena Hosking wrote the founding account of Lance's Panel & Spray in 2021 — a document not written for investors or brokers, but written to capture what the business meant and how it came to be. It is one of the most honest small business founding stories I have read, because it does not hide the desperation of the beginning.

Seven years of financial difficulty before the Ford Fairlane. Drug rehabilitation work done for no money because the kids needed it. A cleaning product business sold at flea markets on weekends. A price of R2,000 pulled from nowhere because Lance had not given a quote in twenty years and had no idea what the real number should be.

And then nine vehicles fixed in a borrowed workshop, word spreading through a mechanic client's phone contacts, a house rented specifically for the garage, fliers on every windscreen from Gardens to the Atlantic Seaboard, and 23 years of building something that quietly closed down a competitor that had been there for decades.

The business being offered for sale at R3.5 million is the same business that started with a R2,000 quote that Lance made up on the spot. The only difference is 23 years of work.

That is what is actually for sale. And whoever buys it is not just buying a panel shop in Goodwood. They are buying the foundation that two people built under conditions most businesses never survive — and the position that foundation has earned over two decades of consistent, excellent work.
